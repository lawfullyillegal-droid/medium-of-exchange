# Legal Definition of Money

## Medium of Exchange

Money is legally defined as a medium of exchange - any item or verifiable record that is generally accepted as payment for goods and services and repayment of debts.

## Legal References

### Federal Reserve Act
- 12 USC § 411: Federal Reserve notes shall be obligations of the United States
- Federal Reserve notes are legal tender for all debts, public charges, taxes, and dues

### Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)
- **UCC § 1-201(24)**: "Money" means a medium of exchange currently authorized or adopted by a domestic or foreign government
- The term includes a monetary unit of account established by an intergovernmental organization or by agreement between two or more countries

### Constitutional Definition
- Article I, Section 8: Congress has power to coin money and regulate the value thereof
- Article I, Section 10: No state shall coin money, emit bills of credit, or make anything but gold and silver coin a tender in payment of debts

## Types of Money

1. **Commodity Money**: Has intrinsic value (gold, silver)
2. **Fiat Money**: Government-issued currency without intrinsic value
3. **Credit Money**: Represents a claim on future money (checks, promissory notes)
4. **Electronic Money**: Digital representation of currency

## Key Characteristics

1. **Medium of Exchange**: Facilitates transactions
2. **Unit of Account**: Provides standard measure of value
3. **Store of Value**: Retains purchasing power over time
4. **Legal Tender**: Accepted for debt payment by law

## Relevant Statutes

- 31 USC § 5103: United States coins and currency (including Federal reserve notes) are legal tender
- 12 USC § 342: Federal Reserve Banks authorized to deal in gold coin and bullion
- 31 USC § 5112: Denominations, specifications, and design of coins
