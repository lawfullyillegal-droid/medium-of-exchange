# UCC Article 3: Negotiable Instruments Guide

## Overview

UCC Article 3 governs negotiable instruments - written promises or orders to pay money that can be transferred to others. This article establishes the rules for creating, transferring, and enforcing these instruments.

---

## What is a Negotiable Instrument?

### Definition (UCC § 3-104)

A negotiable instrument is an unconditional promise or order to pay a fixed amount of money that:
1. Is payable to bearer or to order at the time it is issued or first comes into possession of a holder
2. Is payable on demand or at a definite time
3. Does not state any other undertaking by the person promising or ordering payment

### Six Requirements for Negotiability

1. **In Writing**: Must be in tangible form or electronic record
2. **Signed**: By maker (note) or drawer (draft)
3. **Unconditional Promise or Order**: No conditions can be attached to payment
4. **Fixed Amount of Money**: Must state a definite sum
5. **Payable on Demand or at Definite Time**: Payment timing must be clear
6. **Payable to Order or Bearer**: Must be transferable (exception: checks)

## Types of Negotiable Instruments

### 1. Promissory Notes (UCC § 3-104(e))
- **Definition**: Written promise by one party (maker) to pay money to another party (payee)
- **Two-Party Instrument**: Maker and Payee
- **Examples**: 
  - Personal loans
  - Mortgage notes
  - Student loan notes
  - Corporate bonds

### 2. Drafts/Bills of Exchange (UCC § 3-104(e))
- **Definition**: Written order by one party (drawer) directing another party (drawee) to pay money to a third party (payee)
- **Three-Party Instrument**: Drawer, Drawee, and Payee
- **Examples**:
  - Checks (most common)
  - Bank drafts
  - Money orders
  - Cashier's checks
  - Traveler's checks

### 3. Certificates of Deposit
- **Definition**: Bank's written acknowledgment of receiving money with promise to repay
- **Parties**: Bank (maker) and Depositor (payee)

## Key Parties and Roles

| Role | Definition | Found In |
|------|------------|----------|
| **Maker** | Person who promises to pay (signs note) | Promissory Notes |
| **Drawer** | Person who orders payment | Drafts/Checks |

| **Payee** | Person to whom instrument is initially payable | All Instruments |
| **Holder** | Person in possession with right to enforce | All Instruments |
| **Endorser** | Person who signs instrument for transfer | All Instruments |

## Transfer and Negotiation

### Transfer (UCC § 3-203)
- Delivery of instrument by non-issuer for purpose of giving recipient right to enforce
- Transferee receives rights of transferor
- May be subject to defenses

### Negotiation (UCC § 3-201)
- **To Bearer**: Delivery alone
- **To Order**: Delivery + Endorsement
- Creates potential for Holder in Due Course status

### Types of Endorsements (UCC § 3-204 to 3-206)

1. **Blank Endorsement**: Signature only - converts to bearer paper
2. **Special Endorsement**: "Pay to [Name]" + Signature - remains order paper
3. **Restrictive Endorsement**: "For Deposit Only" + Signature - limits use
4. **Qualified Endorsement**: "Without Recourse" + Signature - limits liability

## Holder in Due Course (HDC)

### Definition (UCC § 3-302)

A holder who takes the instrument:
1. **For Value**: Gave something of value in exchange
2. **In Good Faith**: Honest dealing and observance of reasonable commercial standards
3. **Without Notice** of:
   - Dishonor
   - Overdue status
   - Defenses or claims
   - Unauthorized signatures or alterations

### Rights of HDC (UCC § 3-305)

**Protected Against** (Personal Defenses):
- Breach of contract
- Lack of consideration
- Fraud in the inducement
- Prior payment
- Mental incapacity (unless adjudicated)

**Not Protected Against** (Real Defenses):
- Forgery
- Fraud in the factum (fraud as to the instrument itself)
- Material alteration
- Discharge in bankruptcy
- Minority (if state law makes voidable)
- Illegality that voids the instrument
- Duress, lack of legal capacity, illegality

## Liability on Instruments

### Signature Liability (UCC § 3-401 to 3-415)

**Maker's Liability** (UCC § 3-412):
- Obligated to pay according to terms at time of signing
- Liability continues until statute of limitations expires

**Drawer's Liability** (UCC § 3-414):
- Liable if drawee dishonors
- Must give notice of dishonor
- Can disclaim liability with "without recourse"

**Endorser's Liability** (UCC § 3-415):
- Liable if instrument dishonored
- Entitled to notice of dishonor
- Can disclaim with "without recourse"

**Drawee Not Liable** (UCC § 3-408):
- Drawee (bank) not liable until accepts (certifies)
- Acceptance makes drawee primarily liable

### Warranty Liability (UCC § 3-416, 3-417)

Any person who transfers or presents instrument makes warranties:
1. Entitled to enforce
2. Signatures authentic and authorized
3. Not materially altered
4. No defenses or claims against transferor
5. No knowledge of insolvency proceedings

## Presentment, Dishonor, and Notice

### Presentment (UCC § 3-501)
- **Definition**: Demand for payment or acceptance
- **When**: On due date or on demand
- **How**: To maker, drawee, or other payor
- **Effect**: Triggers liability of secondary parties if dishonored

### Dishonor (UCC § 3-502)
- **Occurs When**:
  - Presentment made and payment/acceptance refused
  - Presentment excused and instrument not paid
- **Effect**: Activates endorser and drawer liability

### Notice of Dishonor (UCC § 3-503)
- **Required**: To hold endorsers and drawers liable
- **Timing**: Within 30 days after dishonor
- **Method**: Any commercially reasonable means
- **Excused**: If cannot be given with reasonable diligence

---

## Defenses and Claims

### Personal Defenses (UCC § 3-305(a)(2))
Effective against ordinary holders but not HDC:
- Breach of warranty
- Lack of consideration
- Fraud in the inducement
- Setoff
- Unauthorized completion

### Real Defenses (UCC § 3-305(a)(1))
Effective against everyone including HDC:
- Infancy (minority)
- Duress, lack of capacity, illegality
- Fraud in the factum
- Discharge in insolvency proceedings
- Forgery or unauthorized signature
- Material alteration

## Practical Tips for Record Keeping

1. **Document Everything**: Keep copies of all instruments and endorsements
2. **Track Transfers**: Maintain chain of title documentation
3. **Timely Presentment**: Present instruments promptly to preserve rights
4. **Notice Requirements**: Send notice of dishonor within 30 days
5. **Holder Status**: Document HDC requirements (value, good faith, no notice)
6. **Warranty Claims**: Preserve evidence of breaches
7. **Statute of Limitations**: 
   - 6 years for notes (UCC § 3-118)
   - 3 years for checks (UCC § 3-118)
   - 6 years for certificates of deposit

## Common Issues and Resolutions

### Issue: Missing Endorsement
- **Solution**: Obtain endorsement or prove transfer by other means (UCC § 3-203)

### Issue: Instrument Dishonored
- **Action**: Send notice within 30 days, pursue collection from endorsers/drawer

### Issue: Suspected Forgery
- **Action**: Stop payment if check, notify bank, preserve evidence
- **Defense**: Available against all parties including HDC

### Issue: Lost or Stolen Instrument
- **Action**: File police report, notify obligor, may require bond (UCC § 3-309)

### Issue: Material Alteration
- **Effect**: Discharges parties to extent of alteration (UCC § 3-407)
- **HDC Rights**: Can enforce according to original terms
