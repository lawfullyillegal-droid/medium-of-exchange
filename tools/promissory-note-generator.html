DEFINITIONS AND LEGAL REFERENCES

Negotiable Instrument (UCC § 3-104): An unconditional promise or order to pay a fixed amount of money, with or without interest, payable to bearer or to order at the time it is issued or first comes into possession of a holder, payable on demand or at a definite time, and does not state any other undertaking by the person promising or ordering payment.

Holder (UCC § 3-301): The person in possession of a negotiable instrument that is payable either to bearer or to an identified person that is the person in possession.

Value (UCC § 3-303): An instrument is issued or transferred for value if: (i) the instrument is issued or transferred for a promise of performance, to the extent the promise has been performed; (ii) the transferee acquires a security interest or other lien in the instrument; (iii) the instrument is issued or transferred as payment of, or as security for, an antecedent claim; (iv) the instrument is issued or transferred in exchange for a negotiable instrument; or (v) the instrument is issued or transferred in exchange for the incurring of an irrevocable obligation to a third party.

Consideration: Something of value given in exchange for a promise or performance. Under common law and UCC principles, consideration is required for a contract to be enforceable. See UCC § 3-303 (Value) for negotiable instruments context.

Maker (UCC § 3-103(a)(7)): A person who signs or is identified in a note as a person undertaking to pay.

Drawer (UCC § 3-103(a)(5)): A person who signs or is identified in a draft as a person ordering payment.

Drawee (UCC § 3-103(a)(4)): A person ordered in a draft to make payment.

Payee (UCC § 3-103(a)(11)): A person to whom an instrument is initially payable.

Constitutional Authority: U.S. Constitution Article I, Section 8 grants Congress power to coin money and regulate its value. Article I, Section 10 prohibits states from coining money or making anything but gold and silver coin a tender in payment of debts.

Legal Tender Statute: 31 USC § 5103 establishes United States coins and currency (including Federal Reserve notes) as legal tender for all debts, public charges, taxes, and dues.

Federal Reserve Act: 12 USC § 411 provides that Federal Reserve notes shall be obligations of the United States and shall be receivable by all national and member banks and Federal Reserve banks and for all taxes, customs, and other public dues.

UCC General Provisions: UCC § 1-201(24) defines "money" as a medium of exchange currently authorized or adopted by a domestic or foreign government. UCC § 1-308 permits reservation of rights without prejudice.
